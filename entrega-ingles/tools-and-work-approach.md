## PART A — Written section

### Overview

Our project is **UniGrades**, a web app that helps university students track their grades
and see their GPA update automatically. But the real goal of our project is bigger than the
app: we want to **teach ordinary people how to build and monetize a startup using AI**, and
UniGrades is the example we use to show it. This section explains the **tools** we used and
the **approach** we followed to turn an idea into a real, published product.

### The tools we used

We chose each tool for a clear reason, always preferring options that a non-expert can
actually use:

- **Kiro (our AI copilot).** This was the center of everything. Instead of writing code
  line by line, we described in plain language what we wanted, and the AI built it while we
  reviewed and corrected. The lesson we want to share is simple: *you no longer need to be a
  programmer to build software; you need to know what you want and explain it clearly.*
- **Next.js.** The framework we used to build the website itself (the pages students see and
  click).
- **Supabase.** Our database, the "memory" of the app. We explain it to people as *an
  organized Excel that lives on the internet, with rules nobody can break.* It stores each
  student's subjects and grades safely.
- **Vercel.** The service that publishes the app online for free, giving it a real web link
  anyone can open from their phone.
- **GitHub.** The place where our code is safely stored and versioned, so the whole team
  works on the same project without losing anything.
- **next-intl.** The tool that lets the app switch between Spanish and English, which also
  helps us reach more users.
- **A shared spreadsheet (Excel).** A simple tool our non-technical teammates could use to
  collect the study programs and subjects, which the AI then turned into real data.

### Our work approach

More than the tools, what made the project work was *how* we organized ourselves:

1. **Problem first, not idea first.** We started from a real frustration (calculating your
   GPA by hand takes hours) instead of inventing a random idea.
2. **Plan before building.** For every feature we first discussed and designed it in words
   (a "plan" step), and only then built it (an "execution" step). Thinking before building
   kept the work organized and avoided mistakes.
3. **Make it work before making it pretty.** We first built a plain, functional version, and
   only later applied the visual design. This let us fix the important things first.
4. **Divide the work by area.** We separated the project into clear parts (the logic and
   data on one side, the visual design on the other) so each teammate could focus without
   breaking someone else's work.
5. **Build in public and document everything.** We wrote down every step, every decision,
   and even every mistake, in real time. That record becomes our videos and our ebook.
6. **Feedback in every phase.** Like the course asks, we reviewed and improved our work at
   each stage instead of leaving everything for the end.

### Why this approach matters (impact)

This way of working is **repeatable and durable**: anyone who follows these tools and this
approach can build their own startup. That is exactly the message of our project, and the
reason our content (videos and an ebook) can keep helping people over time.

## PART B — Oral presentation script (≈1.5–2 minutes)

> Tips: speak slowly and clearly, pause at each numbered point, and look at the audience.
> Practice the tool names out loud: *Kiro, Next.js (say "next jay-ess"), Supabase, Vercel,
> GitHub.*

Hello everyone. My part of the planning phase is **the tools we used and how we worked.**

Our project is UniGrades, an app to track student grades. But our real goal is to **teach
people how to build and monetize a startup using artificial intelligence**, and this app is
our example.

Let me start with the **tools**. The most important one was **Kiro**, our AI copilot.
Instead of writing code ourselves, we described what we wanted in plain words, and the AI
built it while we reviewed it. This is our main message: *you don't need to be a programmer
anymore. You need a clear idea and the ability to explain it.*

We also used **Supabase**, which is our database. The easy way to understand it is: *an
Excel that lives on the internet, with rules nobody can break.* To publish the app online
for free, we used **Vercel**, and to store our code safely as a team, we used **GitHub**.

Now, the **approach**, which matters even more than the tools. We followed five simple
ideas. First, we started from a **real problem**, not a random idea. Second, we always
**planned before building.** Third, we made it **work before making it pretty.** Fourth, we
**divided the work** so each teammate could focus. And fifth, we **documented everything in
real time**, which is what turns our process into videos and an ebook.

Why does this matter? Because this way of working is **repeatable**. Anyone can follow it to
build their own startup. And that is exactly what our project wants to teach.

Thank you.
